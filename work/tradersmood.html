What the chart doesn’t show.

TradersMood · Brand direction · Video podcast · 2026

A trading firm asked me to brand its new video podcast. Every trading show talks about profits and losses. I built this one around a different question: not where the market went, but how that week felt for the person living through it.

What I was asked

A brand for a new video podcast about markets and the people who trade them. The brief asked for the idea, the look, one real example of it in use, and the thinking behind each decision.

What I decided

Talk about feelings, not forecasts. Each episode is built around one guest and one sentence they said. Behind them, a hand-drawn line shows how their week felt.

What I made

The logo, four moods with a colour each, episode covers for YouTube, Instagram and stories, a ready-to-use Figma template, a short vertical video, and the presentation itself as a website.

01 · The brief

Everyone shows the result. Nobody talks about how it feels.

The brief described the show in six words: financial markets, people, ideas, stories, opinions and industry insights. Four of the six are about people. Yet trading podcasts all look the same: big money numbers, gurus, red and green arrows. And the people watching usually trade alone, at home, in front of a screen. So I built the show around the one thing a price chart can’t show: how it felt.

02 · The idea

One guest, one sentence.

I explored three ideas. Two were about the crowd: reading the market’s mood through many traders, and a virtual trading floor for people who trade alone. The third was about one person, and that is the one I kept. Every market show already has an expert making predictions. None of them ask how the week actually felt.

01The momentSomething that happened in the markets recently.
02The personWhere were you that week?
03The storyWhat it did to them, and what they did next.
04The sentenceOne line from the conversation becomes the cover.

One rule for the host: ask how it felt, never where the market is going next.

03 · The logo

Half typed, half handwritten.

Set in a font alone, the name looked like every other brand. So it became two voices: Traders in a clean typeface, the way the show speaks, and (mood) in handwriting between two hand-drawn brackets, the way a guest would write it. The logo never changes. Each episode’s mood appears next to it as a small tag, like “EP. 12 · CALM”.

Ideas I dropped: quote marks in place of the two O’s (they read as the number 66), an empty chair inside the O (it looked like an on/off switch), and a different mood written into the logo every week (then the show had no fixed name).

04 · The mood line

Their week, drawn as a line.

Behind each guest runs a hand-drawn line. A stock chart shows what the market did; this line shows how the week felt. Each of the four moods has its own colour and shape, so you can read an episode’s mood at a glance, even in a small thumbnail.

05 · In the feed

From last place to first.

I placed the cover in a realistic YouTube feed, next to real trading-podcast thumbnails, and asked which one gets noticed first. I asked two AI models separately and also ran an eye-tracking prediction tool. On a dark background, the cover came 6th of 6. When the whole background took the mood’s colour, it came 1st. Two moods were too pale, so I made their colours stronger until they held up as well.

06 · Every format

Four guests, four moods, every format.

Every cover follows the same few rules: the guest’s photo, their sentence with one key word highlighted, the mood line, the logo and the episode tag. A Figma template and a small script produce every size, so a new episode takes a designer about two hours.

07 · Honest limits

A direction, not results yet.

The show hasn’t aired, so there are no real numbers yet. The guests on these covers were made with AI as placeholders; in production, each guest’s real photo gets the same black-and-white treatment. The feed test was a quick check with AI models, not with real viewers. I set the three numbers that would show it works in the first 90 days.
