AI production workflow

Part of the Bingo Bay take-home · 2026

How I would run AI production for a casual games studio, from idea to delivery. AI does the volume at every stage, and one named person signs off each one. Bingo Bay is this workflow run once, and every stage below links to where it happened.

What I was asked

An end-to-end AI production workflow for a casual games studio: the stages, the tools at each, where people stay involved, the bottlenecks, and where it can scale.

What I decided

The stages stay fixed. The judgement is what moves. Every stage gets exactly one human gate with a named owner, and the person there works as an editor, not an operator.

What I made

Nine stages across three phases, each with its tools, what gets automated, what stays manual and the jam to expect. Then I ran an event through it, so none of the stages is theory.

Scope

9 stages, 3 phasesDefine, produce, ship

9 human gatesOne named owner at every stage

3 stages in parallelVisuals, sound and copy run side by side

2 cycles, then ship or killIteration never gets to run forever

The nine stages

Same stages every time. A person at every gate.

AI makes the volume, and a person decides every image a player actually sees. Choosing the one of twelve that ships is the part that matters. Where Bingo Bay ran a stage, its link goes straight to the chapter where it happened.

  1. 01Define

    Ideation

    Thirty scored event concepts per sprint, with trend and competitor scans behind them. Output: a one-page direction doc.

    Human gateThe creative lead locks one direction. Volume is cheap. Direction is not.

    In Bingo BayThe concept: Bingo Bay →

    ToolsClaude, Perplexity, Miro AI, store intel

    AutomatedConcept volume, trend and competitor scans, scoring drafts

    Stays manualFraming the problem, picking the direction

    BottleneckAlignment drift. The one-page doc comes first, with fresh player data in it.

  2. 02Define

    Art direction

    Moodboards and style exploration at volume, narrowed to three concepts. Output: three concepts and one locked art direction page.

    Human gateThe art director signs the lock. The highest-leverage call in the pipeline.

    In Bingo BayThe lock, as tokens →

    ToolsNano Banana Pro, Midjourney, Figma

    AutomatedStyle exploration, moodboard volume

    Stays manualThe lock itself: palette, render, light, cast

    BottleneckDecision fatigue. The shortlist is capped at three.

  3. 03Define

    UX exploration

    Flow drafts and variant screens for the lobby, progression and album. Output: approved flows and wireframes.

    Human gateA game designer checks the loop against the live economy.

    In Bingo BayThe lobby and progression screens →

    ToolsFigma AI, Claude, HTML prototypes

    AutomatedVariant drafts, flow diagrams

    Stays manualEconomy hooks, friction calls, game feel

    BottleneckAI mockups look finished too early. Wireframes come first.

  4. 04Produce

    Visual assets Parallel

    Key art, characters and UI art in batches against the locked tokens, with background removal and cutouts. Output: an approved asset pack.

    Human gateA person curates the batch and picks one of eight to twelve. Never the first output.

    In Bingo BayAll 55 frames and why each lost →The brief every request starts from →

    ToolsNano Banana Pro on Google Flow, Bria, Photoshop

    AutomatedBatch generation, four prompts at a time, background removal

    Stays manualSelection, paint-over, retouching to the lock

    BottleneckConsistency at volume. Locked references, seeds and a template prompt.

  5. 05Produce

    Music and sound Parallel

    Loops, sound effects and voice drafts from a style brief. Output: a cleared audio kit.

    Human gateAudio direction sets the brief and clears the rights. Every loop is judged on a phone speaker.

    In Bingo BayThe loop, and the close call →

    ToolsSuno, ElevenLabs

    AutomatedTrack generation, SFX drafts, voice in several languages

    Stays manualThe mix, the loop point, rights clearance

    BottleneckLicensing. A pre-cleared kit.

  6. 06Produce

    Copy Parallel

    Headlines, buttons, popups and store copy, drafted in bulk and fanned out to every locale. Output: a localised copy matrix.

    Human gateThe copy lead picks and trims. Claims never ship unreviewed.

    In Bingo BayThe lines that shipped and lost →

    ToolsClaude with the voice guide as its system prompt

    AutomatedBulk drafts, locale fan-out

    Stays manualThe final voice, anything legal will read

    BottleneckNative review. Done by market priority, not alphabetically.

  7. 07Ship

    Review and QA

    Automated pre-checks on palette, safe areas, contrast and artefacts, then one structured review. Output: a signed-off batch.

    Human gateSign-off is mandatory: the art director for craft, product for the economy, brand for IP.

    In Bingo BayThe four rules every frame was judged by →

    ToolsSpec-check scripts, LLM vision, Figma review

    AutomatedPalette, safe areas, contrast, artefact detection

    Stays manualCreative judgement on whatever passed

    BottleneckThe review queue, which is the biggest jam of all. Pre-checks and batch approvals.

  8. 08Ship

    Iteration

    Performance data turned into improved variants. Output: the next test batch.

    Human gateData proposes, a person disposes. Someone still owns the winning variant.

    In Bingo BayThe iteration log, with every verdict →

    ToolsThe iteration log, A/B data, fast regeneration

    AutomatedVariant regeneration, data pulls, logging

    Stays manualDeciding what to fix and why

    BottleneckEndless loops. Two cycles at most, then ship it or kill it.

    Loops back to stages 4 to 6. The direction stays locked, and there are two cycles at most.

  9. 09Ship

    Delivery

    Export, naming and a versioned upload. Output: a named, sized, uploaded pack with its decision log.

    Human gateOne release owner confirms the manifest. One person, one checklist.

    In Bingo Bay

    Not part of the take-home. The event stopped at the submission.

    ToolsExport and naming scripts, the asset library

    AutomatedThe export matrix, naming, versioned upload

    Stays manualOne release approval

    BottleneckNone, once it is templated.

Where it jams, and where it compounds

The top four jams

  1. 01

    The review queue. Automated pre-checks and batch approvals, so people only see what passed.

  2. 02

    Consistency at volume. Locked references, seeds and template prompts.

  3. 03

    Rights and compliance. Pre-cleared kits, handled by market priority.

  4. 04

    Endless iteration. Two cycles at most, then ship it or kill it.

Where it gets faster each time

  1. ×N

    Variants from one approved base: skins, seasons, ad matrices.

  2. LIB

    Prompt libraries, so each campaign starts further ahead.

  3. KIT

    Approved kits for audio, voice and UX patterns.

  4. AUTO

    Zero-touch delivery once sign-off is given.
